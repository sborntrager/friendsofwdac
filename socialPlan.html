The Narrative Arc

Story Map: Hook to Invitation.

The site acts as a plotted journey where each beat teaches visitors exactly why this matters and what to do next.

1) HookExhibit Label
Goal: Stop the scroll with a concrete emotional opener.
Content: Hero headline, visual cold-open, one-line promise.
Humor opportunity: A self-aware line that frames the problem.
2) Stakes / Why It MattersExhibit Label
Goal: Explain risk, urgency, and impact.
Content: Problem cards, comparison visuals, short social proof.
Humor opportunity: One meme-style comparison for recall.
3) Proof / CredibilityExhibit Label
Goal: Replace skepticism with evidence.
Content: Testimonials, data snippets, before/after outcomes.
Humor opportunity: Light, brand-safe captioning only.
4) TransformationExhibit Label
Goal: Show what changes for the visitor.
Content: Future-state snapshots, timeline of wins.
Humor opportunity: Comedic analogy tied to learning.
5) Invitation (CTA)Exhibit Label
Goal: Ask clearly and confidently.
Content: CTA cluster, objections FAQ, next-step options.
Humor opportunity: Friendly nudge, never pressure.
The Education Engine

Teach Visitors Until Decisions Feel Easy.

Educational modules make complex topics graspable, memorable, and actionable.

Explainers / Primers

When to use

Early sections to establish baseline understanding. Example headline: "How this actually works in 90 seconds."

Myth vs Fact

Desired outcome

Correct false assumptions with confidence. Example headline: "3 myths slowing progress down."

Quick Wins

When to use

Mid-funnel reassurance. Example headline: "Do this first and see impact this week."

Visual Diagrams

Desired outcome

Turn abstract systems into clear maps. Example headline: "One diagram, full picture."

FAQs That Do Not Suck

When to use

Near CTA to remove blockers. Example headline: "Short answers to real concerns."

Case Studies

Desired outcome

Engagement burst in long pages. Example headline: "Pick your path in 45 seconds."

Viral + Humor Guardrails

Humor Is a Teaching Tool, Not a Detour.

Every joke earns its place by clarifying a point, increasing memorability, or lowering friction.

  1. Must clarify, not distract.
  2. Must be brand-safe in tone and context.
  3. Must reinforce learning or memory.
Meme-Style Comparisons

Why it works

Rapid pattern recognition. Risk note: avoid niche references. Example: "Old way vs clear way."

Reaction GIFs (if allowed)

Why it works

Emotional punctuation. Risk note: overuse undermines credibility. Example: one GIF per major section max.

Punchy Reels / TikToks

Why it works

Reach and replay value. Risk note: keep message-first scripts. Example: 20-second concept breakdown.

Hot Take Micro-Posts

Why it works

Conversation starter. Risk note: avoid controversy without context. Example: "What everyone gets backward."

Comedic Analogies

Why it works

Makes abstraction concrete. Risk note: analogy must map accurately. Example: "Your IA is a museum floor plan."
