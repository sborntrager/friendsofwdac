## 1. Objective

Use the existing brand guidelines to create a website that feels unmistakably custom, visually strong, strategically clear, accessible, and fast.

The revised site must:
- feel rooted in Cottage Grove and WDAC specifically
- use the approved logo, palette, and typography system already established in the moodboard and current working site
- prioritize trust, clarity, and conversion over decorative trend effects
- avoid a generic nonprofit, SaaS, or agency-template feel

## 2. Locked Brand Guardrails

These are not open for reinvention during the next pass.

### Logo
- Keep the existing horizontal WDAC logo now used in `site/public/index_working.html`.
- Do not redesign or replace the logo system.

### Color Palette
- Onyx: `#353935`
- Ultra White: `#FAFDFF`
- Venice Blue: `#0F5C86`
- Crazy Blue: `#2C93C5`
- Boho Yellow: `#F2C14E`
- Red Shimmer / Coral: `#F45B5B`

Use colors by role, not decoration:
- Blues carry trust, structure, and navigation
- Coral marks the strongest actions
- Yellow works as emphasis, warmth, and accent
- White and near-white backgrounds keep the site readable and civic, not flashy

### Typography
- Keep the approved site stack already in use:
  - `Fraunces` for display moments
  - `Montserrat` for headings, labels, and UI structure
  - `Inter` for body copy and supporting text
- Do not introduce new fonts.

### Image System
- Use real WDAC and Cottage Grove imagery whenever possible.
- Keep the blend of present-day pool life, archival/history imagery, and local civic context.
- Preserve visible photo/source credits where needed.
- Continue using the pre-made faded `BOX_*` images as a branded navigation-image system.

### Voice and Tone
- Warm
- Neighborly
- Credible
- Civic
- Practical
- Specific without sounding academic

Do not drift into:
- corporate fundraising language
- generic nonprofit boilerplate
- vague motivational copy

## 4. Brand DNA Summary

### Traits to amplify
- Community stewardship
- Real-world usefulness
- Local history with present-day energy

### Traits to avoid
- Generic nonprofit polish
- Corporate institutional stiffness
- Repetitive card-grid sameness

### Tone in plain language
This site should sound like informed, caring neighbors protecting an important public asset and making it easy for others to help.

### Visual tension pairings
- Civic + warm
- Historic + active
- Trustworthy + human

### Primary emotional goal
Make visitors feel that WDAC is a beloved local institution worth protecting right now.

### Primary trust signal
Real proof: real photos, real local history, real forms, real quotes, real community context.

### What makes WDAC different
This is not a generic nonprofit or a commercial aquatic brand. It sits at the intersection of community health, local memory, public access, and grassroots support for a specific hometown asset.

### Core positioning sentence
This site should feel like a beloved community institution being actively championed, not like a nonprofit template.

## 5. Core Art-Direction Concept

### Concept name
Living Civic Archive

### Concept summary
The site should feel like an active community story in motion: present-day pool life, civic support, and local history layered together in a clear, contemporary interface.

### Compositional style
- Editorial rather than templated
- Large visual beats mixed with quieter utility blocks
- Intentional variation in section density
- Image-led navigation where appropriate

### Image treatment
- Real photography first
- Mix of present-day, archival, and civic imagery
- Faded `BOX_*` images remain the branded navigation-tile treatment
- Visible credits remain part of the system, not an afterthought

### Motion style
- Restrained and useful
- Soft reveals, layered entrances, subtle hover movement
- No flashy animation libraries or heavy effects

### Spacing rhythm
- Alternate spacious editorial sections with tighter utility sections
- Avoid equal-height, equal-weight section stacking

### Shape language
- Rounded but not bubbly
- Calm curves, soft containers, and wave-informed transitions where useful

### Icon style
- Minimal and purposeful
- Avoid trendy illustration packs or startup-style icon clutter

### Texture / motif
- Water flow and wave logic
- Archival-to-present contrast
- Light atmospheric backgrounds, not novelty effects

### Signature visual move
Use branded image-led pathways and at least one memorable editorial band per key page instead of relying on a repeated card grid.

### Signature content move
Proof-first community storytelling using real quotes, forms, history, and visible support context.

### Rejection test
Reject any concept that could be dropped onto twenty other nonprofits with only a logo swap.

## 7. Visual System Rules

### Color roles
- Background: Ultra White and very light blue atmospherics
- Surface: white and soft-tinted white
- Primary action: Coral
- Trust / structure: Venice Blue and deeper blues
- Accent: Boho Yellow
- Body text: Onyx
- Supporting/divider tone: light blue-gray and subtle blue borders

### Type roles
- Display: `Fraunces`
- Heading/UI structure: `Montserrat`
- Body/supporting copy: `Inter`
- Eyebrows/meta: `Montserrat`, uppercase, tight and restrained
- CTA/button text: `Montserrat` or strong `Inter` weight, depending on control size

### Spacing rules
- Build in section-to-section rhythm changes
- Keep large hero and story sections more spacious
- Keep utility sections tighter and more direct
- Avoid identical vertical padding on every section

### Border radius, lines, shadows, cards
- Rounded corners stay soft and calm
- Borders should be light and structural, not decorative
- Shadows should support depth lightly, not create a glossy UI
- Cards should be used selectively, not as the default answer to every content type

### Image rules
- Full-bleed for major moments
- Framed when crediting or when the image needs editorial structure
- Cropped intentionally for mood and focus
- Use the `BOX_*` treatments for navigational surfaces, not generic promo blocks

### Button rules
- Coral button is reserved for the strongest action on the page
- Blue and ghost actions support secondary paths
- Do not let every section end in the same big pill button

### Link and emphasis styling
- Use text links and lighter directional links where appropriate
- Reserve buttons for actual conversion actions
- Emphasis should come from hierarchy and copy, not excessive badges

### Navigation behavior
- Desktop: direct and calm, with clear CTA hierarchy
- Mobile: obvious menu, thumb-friendly tap targets, no clutter
- Mobile nav should feel intentionally composed, not collapsed leftovers

### Reusable section families
Use these families across the site, but vary density and composition:
- Story hero with proof
- Editorial proof block
- Image-led path/navigation band
- Archival plus present-day story section
- Utility downloads/forms block
- Conversion close section

Do not let all of them resolve into the same white card treatment.

## 8. Homepage Strategy

### Page goal
Orient first-time visitors, establish trust quickly, and move them toward donate, join, or deeper exploration.

### Visitor intent
- What is Friends of WDAC?
- Why does it matter?
- Why should I trust it?
- What should I do next?

### Main question the page must answer
Why is this community effort real, important, and worth supporting right now?

### Primary CTA
Donate

### Secondary CTA
Become a Friend

### Required proof near the top
- real imagery
- community/history context
- quote, facts, or other visible trust signals

### Emotional job
Reassure and activate

### Homepage section goals
- Hero: state the mission and emotional position in under one screen
- Trust/action band: present the clearest next steps
- Intro/proof block: explain who Friends of WDAC is
- Pathways block: let users move by interest, not by generic menu logic
- Story band: show the mix of present-day, history, and civic pride
- Closing utility/conversion section: sign-up, contact, docs, next actions

### Homepage anti-template rule
The homepage cannot read like:
- hero
- three cards
- features grid
- testimonial strip
- CTA footer

It must feel like a custom civic story with utility embedded into it.

## 10. Copy and Messaging Rules

- Keep content people-first and specific.
- Use real WDAC language, dates, names, and evidence where available.
- Avoid filler paragraphs written just to complete a section.
- Avoid generic headings like `Solutions`, `Services`, or `About Us`.
- Prefer headings that sound civic, local, and human.
- CTA labels should be specific whenever possible:
  - `Donate now`
  - `Become a Friend`
  - `Download membership form`
  - `View timeline`

## 11. Anti-Template Rules

Reject any page if any of the following are true:
- It could fit another nonprofit with almost no changes.
- It says nothing visually specific about WDAC.
- Every section uses the same box, spacing, and composition.
- The layout falls back to a default marketing stack.
- The design relies on trendy effects more than message and trust.
- Mobile looks like a compressed version of desktop.
- The page is attractive but unclear about what to do next.

Specific WDAC anti-template reminders:
- Do not overuse pill badges
- Do not turn every content type into matching cards
- Do not use startup-style gradients or glass effects
- Do not over-polish the site until it stops feeling local and real

## 12. Accessibility Checklist

- Check color contrast for all text and CTAs
- Check keyboard navigation
- Check visible focus states
- Check heading hierarchy
- Check form labels and status/error clarity
- Check alt text for meaningful images
- Check tap target sizes on mobile
- Check readable text sizing and spacing
- Keep file downloads clearly labeled
- Align with WCAG 2.2 expectations where possible

## 13. Performance Checklist

- Compress and size all images appropriately
- Avoid heavy media above the fold unless truly necessary
- Keep motion lightweight and CSS-first where possible
- Avoid unnecessary third-party scripts
- Protect Core Web Vitals:
  - LCP: hero content loads fast
  - INP: forms, menu, and interactions respond quickly
  - CLS: wave bars, images, and tile blocks do not jump during load

## 14. Mobile-First Reality Check

- Recompose the hero intentionally for mobile
- Keep primary actions visible without crowding
- Make menu behavior obvious and easy to use
- Re-check image crops for all key page visuals
- Preserve one memorable visual moment per key page on mobile too
- Keep utility pages especially easy to scan on smaller screens
